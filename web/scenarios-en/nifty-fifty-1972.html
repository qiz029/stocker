Historical scenario

1972 · Nifty Fifty

1972–1975 · ~875 trading days · 14 tickers · Death by a thousand cuts, plus an oil crisis

What actually happened

In the early 1970s, US institutions crowded into 50 "one-decision" blue chips — McDonald's, Xerox, Polaroid and Disney among them. The logic was simple: these companies were so good that any price was worth paying to hold them forever. At the 1972 peak, the Nifty Fifty traded at an average P/E above 40×.

Then came the cascade: rate hikes, inflation, Watergate, and in October 1973 the oil crisis, when the OPEC embargo roughly quadrupled crude prices. From January 1973 to October 1974, the S&P 500 fell about 48%; many of the "buy at any price" darlings lost seventy to ninety percent from their highs.

The lesson is still quoted today: a great company is not the same as a great stock — and valuation debts always come due.

Inside Stocker

This scenario covers 1972 to mid-1975: about 875 trading days and 14 aliased tickers — the most grinding of the four. There is no single-day terror, only a slow bleed that drains your loan interest, your option premiums and your patience, one session at a time. The headlines will keep preaching faith; telling signal from belief is the core exam of this timeline.
